        <h3>My Leads — the leads assigned to you</h3>

        <ol className="steps">
          <li>Open <b>My Leads</b>. Add a new one by hand if someone calls in directly.</li>
          <li>Move each lead through its stages as you work it: <code>New</code> → <code>Contacted</code> → <code>In Progress</code> → <code>Won</code> or <code>Lost</code>.</li>

      <article className="usecase" id="staff-pipeline">
        <h3>Team pipeline, and converting a Won lead</h3>
        <div className="who-sees">Who sees this: <b>Leader, Group Leader, Admin</b> — a Rater doesn't get this page (they have My Leads instead).</div>
        <p><b>Team Leads CRM</b> (the Pipeline board) shows the whole team's leads — or filter to one person's — as either a drag-and-drop board grouped by stage, or a sortable table. From here you can reassign a lead, leave a note, and:</p>

          <li>Move a lead to <code>Won</code>.</li>
          <li>Open it and choose <b>Convert to Order</b>.</li>

        <h3>Selling over the phone</h3>
        <div className="who-sees">Who sees this: <b>every staff level</b>.</div>
        <p>If a customer calls in instead of buying online, use <b>Buy Plan for Customer</b>: pick their existing account or create a new one, pick the service and plan, and it creates the order, the invoice, and a payment link in one go — including their login, if they're new.</p>

      <article className="usecase" id="staff-orders">

        <div className="who-sees">Who sees this: staff see orders assigned to them plus their reporting chain's; admin sees every order in the org.</div>
        <p>Open <b>Orders</b> for the full list. Use the <b>Ready to Work</b> filter to jump straight to orders that are paid, have their documents verified, and aren't finished yet — the ones actually ready for you to act on. Open an order to:</p>
        <ul className="plain">
          <li>Move it through its process stages (advance it with <b>Move to &lt;next stage&gt;</b>, or cancel it).</li>
          <li>Review uploaded documents and mark each one verified or rejected (with a note) — or use <b>Mark all verified</b> once everything checks out.</li>

      </article>

      <article className="usecase" id="staff-invoice-crm">
        <h3>Invoice CRM — billing that isn't tied to a purchase</h3>
        <div className="who-sees">Who sees this: any staff or admin, scoped to their own org — never shown to customers.</div>
        <p>For an order that came from an actual purchase, invoices are already handled automatically (see Order Invoices below). <b>Invoice CRM</b> is for everything else — a GST invoice for something ad-hoc, a non-GST or export invoice, a proforma, or a credit/debit note against an existing invoice.</p>
        <ol className="steps">
          <li>Open Invoice CRM and start <b>+ Generate Invoice</b>.</li>
          <li>Pick the invoice type, the customer (or add a new one inline), and fill in place-of-supply and line items from your product/service catalog.</li>
          <li>Add any charges (freight, packing) and a discount if needed, then generate.</li>
        </ol>
        <div className="callout">
          <div className="callout-label">How GST is calculated</div>
          <p>If your company and the customer are in the <b>same state</b>, tax splits evenly into CGST + SGST. In <b>different states</b>, it's all IGST instead. Either way the total tax is the same — only how it's split changes.</p>
        </div>
        <div className="callout">
          <div className="callout-label">If TDS applies</div>
          <p>TDS is calculated on the pre-tax value of the invoice, never on the GST-inclusive total — GST charged on an invoice was never part of the income TDS applies to.</p>
        </div>
        <p>This page also has each customer's running account statement (every invoice and payment, with a running balance), the full invoice list, and recurring invoice templates that generate themselves on a schedule.</p>

      <article className="usecase">
        <h3>Order Invoices — recording a payment</h3>
        <div className="who-sees">Who sees this: staff see invoices they created plus their reporting chain's; admin sees every invoice.</div>
        <p>Open <b>Order Invoices</b>, find the invoice, and use <b>Record Payment</b> for a cash, bank, UPI, or cheque payment the customer made outside of online checkout — partial payments are fine, the balance and status update as you go (<code>sent</code> → <code>partially paid</code> → <code>paid</code>).</p>

        <div className="who-sees">Who sees this: every staff level — always your own only, never a customer.</div>
        <ul className="plain">

          <li><b>Leave</b> — apply for leave, see your balance per leave type (carried forward, accrued this year, used), and withdraw a still-pending request or cancel an approved one before it starts. Once it's started, it can't be cancelled — you were actually out for it.</li>

          <div className="callout-label">Running out of balance?</div>

          <li><b>Analytics</b> (Group Leader and Admin only) — company-wide charts: revenue trend, leads funnel, order status breakdown, staff leaderboard.</li>

          <li><b>Documents</b> — upload your own ID/employment documents. A manager can also see and upload for their own reporting chain; admin sees and manages everyone's.</li>

          <li><b>Account Requests</b> — everyone can see the queue of pending customer/staff account requests, but only admin can actually approve one. Already-decided ones are on <b>Reviewed Account Requests</b>.</li>

          <li><b>Delegated Access</b> — hand one specific staff member access to one normally admin-only tool (Employees, Payroll, Payslips, HR Letters, Reports, and more) without promoting them to admin, which would unlock everything. Set it to expire on a date, or leave it permanent.</li>

      <article className="usecase" id="admin-payroll">
        <h3>Payroll</h3>
        <ol className="steps">
          <li>On the <b>Payroll</b> page, set each employee's salary structure — Basic, HRA, Special Allowance, Bonus, Incentives.</li>
          <li>Pick a month and year, and click <b>Generate Salary</b> — it creates a payslip for everyone with a structure who doesn't already have one for that month.</li>
        </ol>
        <div className="callout">
          <div className="callout-label">How the numbers are worked out</div>
          <p>
            <b>Loss of Pay (LOP)</b> — any working day with neither a check-in nor approved paid leave counts against you; a declared holiday or a weekend never does. A full month of absence deducts the full month's pay, not a fraction of it.<br /><br />
            <b>PF</b> — 12% of Basic, capped at the ₹15,000 statutory wage ceiling.<br />
            <b>ESI</b> — 0.75% of gross pay, only if gross is ₹21,000 or under.<br />
            <b>Professional Tax</b> — a state levy; the slab depends on the company's own state (some states charge none at all).<br /><br />
            All of these apply to pay actually <em>earned</em> after LOP, not the full structure — you're never taxed on money you didn't receive.
          </p>

        <p>See every payslip ever generated, for anyone, on <b>Payslips</b> (the company-wide register — separate from an individual's own My Payslips).</p>

          <div className="callout-label">Do this first</div>
          <p><b>Business Settings</b> — address, GSTIN, PAN, state, and at least one payment method (bank details or UPI) — is mandatory. Every invoice-generating action (Buy Plan for Customer, converting a lead, Invoice CRM) is blocked with a clear error until this is filled in. Editing a price here only changes that autofill default; it never changes what a customer actually pays through checkout.</p>

          <li><b>Email Failures</b> — a log of transactional emails (mostly new-account passwords) that failed to send, so you can follow up with that person by hand instead of it silently vanishing.</li>
          <li><b>HR Letters</b> — generate an offer letter, appointment letter, or similar from a template for any employee.</li>
